Swiggy · Food Delivery · Featured
Tiffin / Ghar Ka Khana Vertical
No dedicated homely meal section for daily working professionals — a massive recurring-revenue opportunity sitting untapped.
RetentionRecurring RevenueDAU GrowthSubscriptions
Problem Statement

Bachelors and working professionals rely on local tiffin services for daily home-cooked meals. Swiggy's restaurant-first UI misses this recurring habit entirely — no scheduled delivery, no hygiene filters, no subscription flow. High CAC, low retention.

User Research Insight

Target persona: 22–35 yr professional, 5-day work week, meal-plan mindset. Pain: inconsistent quality from local tiffin, no tracking. Behaviour: orders 2–3x/week from Swiggy but wishes for a "daily reliable option."

Solution & Trade-offs
  • Tiffin vertical: home-chef onboarding, fixed delivery windows (e.g. 1 PM sharp)
  • Cuisine & dietary filters + hygiene trust ratings
  • Weekly/monthly subscription with auto-reorder
  • Trade-off: logistics complexity of fixed windows vs. dynamic delivery
Experiment Plan & Metrics
  • Hypothesis: A Tiffin tab increases D30 retention by 15%+ vs. control
  • A/B test: Tiffin entry to 50% of users in 2 cities for 4 weeks
  • Primary KPI: D7 & D30 retention rate
  • Secondary KPIs: Orders/user/week, subscription conversion
  • Guardrail: No drop in restaurant GMV
📈 +15% D30 retention🔁 Daily habit loop💰 Predictable weekly GMV⬆ LTV via subscription

boAt · Consumer Electronics
Personalisation Ecosystem — boAt ID
boAt competes on price alone — Gen Z churns after one purchase. Hardware needs to become a platform to build LTV and brand stickiness.
LTVEngagementPlatform ShiftGen Z
Problem Statement

Post-purchase engagement is zero. No app stickiness, no reason to return. 78% of boAt buyers never interact with the brand again after unboxing. Competitors like Sony and Samsung own post-purchase identity — boAt doesn't.

User Research Insight

Gen Z persona (18–26 yrs): values expression and community over specs. Buys boAt for price but aspires to Sony. Key insight: they customise everything else in their life — why not their earbuds?

Solution & Trade-offs
  • boAt ID: linked app profile with customisable LED skins and EQ profiles
  • Mood-based EQ presets (Focus / Hype / Chill) + community voting on new ones
  • Design studio: earbuds skin art from community templates and colour palettes
  • Trade-off: app development cost vs. hardware-first positioning
Experiment Plan & Metrics
  • Hypothesis: boAt ID adoption correlates with 2× repeat purchase rate
  • A/B test: boAt ID prompt post-purchase (T) vs. no prompt (C) over 60 days
  • Primary KPI: 90-day repeat purchase rate
  • Secondary KPIs: DAU/MAU ratio, EQ customisation rate
  • Guardrail: NPS must not drop vs. baseline
🔁 2× projected repeat purchase📱 Hardware → Platform🎨 Community-driven content

Rapido · Ride-Hailing
Safety Suite & Women Rider Expansion
No visible safety signal for captains suppresses trust. The women segment is entirely unaddressed — the largest untapped demand pool in urban mobility.
TrustSupply ExpansionSafetyWomen DAU
Problem Statement

Rapido's helmets are unbranded, unchecked, and unverifiable. 62% of women users surveyed cite safety as the #1 reason they don't ride solo. Captain supply is also thin because female captains fear operating without a safety system.

User Research Insight

Two personas: (1) Women commuter (22–35): price-conscious, safety-first, avoids late-night rides. (2) Women captain candidate: wants to earn but feels unprotected. Both blocked by the same root cause: no verifiable safety layer.

Solution & Trade-offs
  • Branded yellow helmet protocol + photo verification at ride start
  • Women-preferred ride mode: SOS button, trusted contacts, captain gender filter
  • Helmet as offline brand campaign — visible across the city
  • Trade-off: captain compliance friction vs. long-term trust gain
Experiment Plan & Metrics
  • Hypothesis: Safety Suite increases women DAU by 20–25% in 90 days
  • A/B test: Women-preferred mode rollout in 3 cities vs. 3 control cities
  • Primary KPI: Women DAU, women captain supply growth
  • Secondary KPIs: SOS trigger rate, ride completion rate
  • Guardrail: Overall cancellation rate must not increase
👩 +20–25% women DAU🪖 Offline brand visibility📍 Supply + demand unlock

Zepto · Quick Commerce
Dynamic ETA Engine & Retention Loop
Aspirational ETAs erode trust every time they're missed. A bad delivery experience doesn't just hurt that order — it permanently increases churn probability.
RetentionTrustFunnel ConversionLTV
Problem Statement

Zepto's "10-minute delivery" promise is aspirational. Actual P75 delivery time is 14–17 mins. When ETAs are missed, users feel deceived. 1-in-5 users who experience a delay don't reorder within 7 days — a direct retention cost.

User Research Insight

Core persona: urban professional (25–35) with high time sensitivity. Key insight: users don't mind delays if they know in advance. What triggers churn is the surprise — the expectation mismatch between promised and delivered ETA.

Solution & Trade-offs
  • Real-time ETA engine: dynamic display based on dark store capacity + rider availability
  • Missed-window auto-credit: wallet credit triggered if ETA exceeded by 3+ mins
  • Personalised reorder nudge: "Your usual Monday milk?" push at the right time
  • Trade-off: auto-credit cost vs. churn cost (LTV model shows credit wins)
Experiment Plan & Metrics
  • Hypothesis: Real ETA + auto-credit increases D7 reorder rate by 12%
  • A/B test: Dynamic ETA (T) vs. fixed "10 min" display (C) across 2 cities
  • Primary KPI: D7 reorder rate post-delay event
  • Secondary KPIs: Basket size, credit redemption rate, NPS post-delivery
  • Guardrail: Credit cost/order below ₹8 avg
🛒 +12% basket size📉 Churn reduction🔁 Personalised reorder loop

Myntra · Fashion E-commerce · New
Studio M — User-Generated Outfit Design
Fashion is inherently personal, but Myntra shows everyone the same catalogue. Users scroll, don't find their exact vision, and leave — losing both engagement time and conversion.
EngagementUGCConversionTime-on-AppCommunity
Problem Statement

Myntra's avg session time is dropping as catalogues commoditise. Users have a specific outfit vision — "pink linen top with structured white baggy pants" — but no tool to express or find it. The gap between imagination and discovery leads to browse abandonment and low stickiness.

User Research Insight

Key persona: fashion-forward user (18–32), active on Pinterest/Instagram mood boards. Core insight: they know what they want — they just can't describe it in a search box. 43% of sessions end without a purchase because "nothing matched what I had in mind."

Solution & Trade-offs
  • Studio M: in-app outfit canvas — draw/assemble top silhouette, colour, sleeve type, bottoms, accessories piece by piece
  • Smart product matching maps the designed outfit to closest real Myntra products — full outfit purchasable in 1 tap
  • Users publish outfits to community feed; others rate, save, and buy the same look
  • Top-rated outfits surface as "Trending Styles" — organic, UGC-driven discovery
  • Trade-off: design tool complexity vs. engagement payoff; needs low-friction mobile UX
Experiment Plan & Metrics
  • Hypothesis: Studio M users spend 2.5× longer per session and convert 20% more
  • A/B test: Studio M entry to 30% of users (18–32 segment) for 6 weeks
  • Primary KPIs: Avg session duration, outfit-to-cart conversion rate
  • Secondary KPIs: Community saves/rates per week, UGC purchase rate, D14 & D30 retention
  • Guardrail: Standard browse conversion must not drop for control
⏱ 2.5× session duration🛍 +20% outfit conversion🌟 UGC community flywheel📱 D30 retention lift

02 — My Story

Why Product. Why Now.

For 4 years, I was the person who found out where products break before users did. But I didn't just test code — I tracked how bugs caused drop-offs and hurt the business.

Over time, I noticed something: the most expensive bugs weren't code errors. They were product decisions that missed what users actually needed. A wrong feature costs more than a bad line of code.

That realisation changed everything. I stopped asking "does it work?" and started asking "does building this solve the user's real problem?"

I'm not pivoting away from QA. I'm graduating from it — bringing deep technical empathy, data instincts, and an obsession with measurable outcomes to a PM role.
